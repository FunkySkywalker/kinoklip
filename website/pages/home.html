A free video editor for Linux, laid out like DaVinci Resolve

Kinoklip has the pages, menus and keyboard shortcuts of Resolve, so most Resolve tutorials carry over. It is free software written in C++ with Qt and FFmpeg, and it includes H.264, H.265 and AAC.

Seven pages, as in Resolve

The buttons along the bottom of the window. Shift+2 opens Media, up to Shift+8 for Deliver.

Media
Look through your files and bring them into the project. Bins, smart bins, metadata.
Cut
A quick editor for first versions, with a source tape and two timelines.
Edit
The main editor: tracks, trims, transitions, titles, keyframes.
Compositing
Node graphs for pictures made of several layers, masks and keyers.
Color
Wheels, curves, a qualifier, windows and LUTs, as a node graph per clip.
Audio
Track levels, meters, equaliser, dynamics and voice-over recording.
Deliver
Presets, a render queue and per-clip export, in H.264 or H.265 with AAC.

Resolve on Linux, and where Kinoklip differs

Resolve runs on Linux, but support is minimal: a short list of supported distributions, NVIDIA first, and no H.264 or H.265 in the free version. Kinoklip aims at the people that leaves out.

HardwareAny GPU with OpenGL 3.3, integrated graphics included. Decoding through VA-API on Intel and AMD.
CodecsH.264, H.265 and AAC for import and export, with no extra purchase.
RecordingPipeWire and PulseAudio, on Wayland and X11.
LicenceGPL-3.0. The source and the issue tracker are on GitHub.
MaturityBeta. Resolve is far more complete, and its makers are paid to work on it full time.

On Windows or macOS, use Resolve. Kinoklip is not available there, and its free version is excellent. Buying Studio supports the people who made the workflow Kinoklip copies.
